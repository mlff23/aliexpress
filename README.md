## What AliExpress Is (And Isn't) in 2026

AliExpress is owned by Alibaba Group, but it's not Alibaba. Think of it as the international consumer-facing version. Small and medium-sized businesses in China (and increasingly other countries) list products, you buy them at wholesale-adjacent prices, and they ship directly to you.

The platform covers basically everything: electronics, fashion, home goods, beauty products, sports equipment, toys, car accessories. If it exists as a manufactured product, someone's probably selling it on AliExpress.

Here's what makes it different from Amazon or other marketplaces: you're buying closer to the source. The prices reflect that. So does the shipping time in many cases.

## The MyCoins System: Free Money If You Actually Use It

This is probably the most underutilized feature on the platform.

AliExpress has a virtual currency called MyCoins. Every 100 coins = $1 in discount credit. You earn coins by:

- **Daily check-ins**: Open the app, visit the Coins page, get 20-70 coins depending on your streak
- **Playing in-app games**: Merge Boss, GoGo Match, Prize Land (yes, really)
- **Completing simple tasks**: Browsing sponsored items, adding to cart
- **Making purchases**: Some orders come with coin bonuses
- **Writing reviews**: Product feedback earns you coins

The catch? Coins expire after 6 months. And not every product accepts coin discounts.

But here's the play: if you're already shopping on AliExpress semi-regularly, opening the app daily for 30 seconds to collect coins is basically free money. Over a month, you can easily accumulate 1,500-2,000 coins ($15-20) without buying anything.

Then during a sale, you stack your coins with promo codes and flash deals. That's when the savings compound.

## Shipping: The Real Story

This is where AliExpress gets interesting, and sometimes frustrating.

**Choice Items**
Products marked with a "Choice" badge are handled differently. These ship from local warehouses in many cases, or use AliExpress's own logistics network. Delivery times: typically 6-15 days to the US, 10-20 days to Europe.

One shopper mentioned receiving a Choice order in 6 days. Another reported consistent 2-week deliveries.

**Standard Shipping**
This is the free option for most items. Delivery estimate: 20-60 days, sometimes longer to remote areas. I've seen recent reports of items taking 75+ days to reach certain locations.

Be honest with yourself: if you need something by a specific date, don't rely on free shipping from China. Pay for faster options or buy elsewhere.

**Local Delivery Options**
AliExpress has been pushing "Local Shipping" more aggressively, where products ship from warehouses in your region. Faster, more reliable, but product selection is more limited and prices are sometimes higher.

## The Product Quality Question

Let's be direct: quality on AliExpress is wildly variable.

You can find genuinely good products at excellent prices. You can also get cheap junk that breaks in a week. The platform hosts over 70,000 suppliers, and they're not all operating at the same standard.

**How to avoid garbage:**
1. **Check seller ratings religiously**: Look for stores with 95%+ positive feedback and thousands of orders
2. **Read reviews obsessively**: Focus on photo reviews from buyers who received the product
3. **Watch for "too good to be true" pricing**: $30 Nike shoes that look identical to $150 retail pairs? They're replicas
4. **Stick to Choice items when possible**: These go through additional vetting
5. **Use the "Orders" filter in reviews**: See products from highest number of completed orders

Several long-term users mentioned they've been shopping on AliExpress for 5-10 years without major issues by following these rules.

## The Customer Service Reality Check

This is where the platform struggles, and it's important to know upfront.

Based on recent reviews across multiple platforms (Trustpilot, Sitejabber, BBB), customer service complaints are common:

- **Dispute resolution can be painful**: Many reports of denied refund claims even with photo/video evidence
- **Language barriers**: Support responses are sometimes automated or poorly translated
- **Return shipping costs**: For defective items, you often have to pay return shipping to China
- **Protection periods**: Items marked "delivered" but never received create disputes that favor sellers

One reviewer described spending "two hours per day for weeks" trying to resolve a lost package issue. Another mentioned being denied a refund despite the delivery company confirming the parcel was lost.

The buyer protection exists, but it's not as robust as Amazon's no-questions-asked return policy.

**The safer approach:**
- Only buy what you're okay potentially losing if disputes fail
- Use PayPal when possible (extra layer of buyer protection)
- Choose "Choice" items for better seller accountability
- Document everything with unboxing videos for expensive purchases
- Check if the seller has a return policy before ordering
